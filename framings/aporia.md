# Inconsistency & aporia

- **Domain:** Epistemic
- **When:** Socratic elenchus
- **Who:** Socrates; Vlastos; Hamblin
- **Remedy implied:** Elenchus, obligational games, commitment-tracking.
- **Methods presupposing it:** [Socratic elenchus](../approaches/elenchus.md), [Platonic dialectic](../approaches/platonic_dialectic.md), [Tibetan monastic debate](../approaches/tibetan.md), [Scholastic disputation](../approaches/disputatio.md), [Obligational disputation](../approaches/obligationes.md), [Street epistemology](../approaches/street_epist.md), [Formal dialectic & dialogue types](../approaches/formal_dialectic.md)

The disagreement that matters is within a person, among beliefs they already hold. An interlocutor is shown that their commitments cannot all stand. The external opponent is only the occasion for internal disagreement. Formal dialectic later makes this precise as a commitment store that has to stay consistent.
