# Interests beneath positions

- **Domain:** Interest
- **When:** Follett 1925; Fisher & Ury 1981
- **Who:** Mary Parker Follett; Roger Fisher; William Ury
- **Remedy implied:** Ask why; invent options; integrative bargaining.
- **Methods presupposing it:** [Deliberative rhetoric](../approaches/rhetoric.md), [Court policy debate](../approaches/saltiron.md), [Parliamentary debate](../approaches/parliament.md), [Palaver & consensus](../approaches/palaver.md), [Integration](../approaches/follett.md), [Strategic bargaining](../approaches/game_bargaining.md), [Mediation](../approaches/mediation.md), [Principled negotiation](../approaches/principled.md), [Deliberative polling](../approaches/delib_polling.md)

What people say they want, their positions, is a strategy for what they need, their interests. Positions are often incompatible where the interests behind them are not. Disagreement is about allocation, and allocation is a design problem.

## Sources
- Follett, Mary Parker. “Constructive Conflict” (1925). In *Dynamic Administration: The Collected Papers of Mary Parker Follett*, edited by Henry C. Metcalf and L. Urwick. New York: Harper, 1940.
- Fisher, Roger, and William Ury. *Getting to Yes: Negotiating Agreement Without Giving In*. Boston: Houghton Mifflin, 1981.
