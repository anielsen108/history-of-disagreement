# Burdens of judgment

- **Domain:** Epistemic
- **When:** Rawls 1993 (anticipated by ʿilm al-khilāf)
- **Who:** John Rawls
- **Remedy implied:** Toleration; public reason; deliberation without expectation of convergence.
- **Methods presupposing it:** [Science of juristic disagreement](../approaches/khilaf.md), [Deliberative polling](../approaches/delib_polling.md), [Citizens’ assemblies](../approaches/citizens.md), [AI-mediated deliberation](../approaches/ai_mediated.md)

Reasonable people disagree without any of them being irrational or ill-willed. Evidence is complex and conflicting, and people weigh the same considerations differently. Concepts are vague at the edges. A person’s whole experience of life shapes their judgment. Normative considerations pull both ways, and every institution must select among values. Rawls calls reasonable pluralism the normal result of human reason exercised under free institutions.
