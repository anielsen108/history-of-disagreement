# Each the measure

- **Domain:** Epistemic
- **When:** Protagoras, c. 450 BCE
- **Who:** Protagoras, as reported by Plato
- **Remedy implied:** Persuasion; argue both sides; change how things appear.
- **Methods presupposing it:** [Antilogic](../approaches/antilogic.md)

Protagoras holds that a human being is the measure of all things. On Plato’s reading, whatever appears to a person is so for that person. Two people who disagree are then both right, each for themselves, and no further fact decides between them. Plato’s Protagoras still ranks opinions: one can be better than another, meaning more useful, though none is truer. On this view the skilled speaker changes how things appear, to a patient or to a city.

> “Of all things the measure is man: of the things that are, that they are, and of the things that are not, that they are not.” — Protagoras, DK 80 B1, in Plato, Theaetetus 152a
