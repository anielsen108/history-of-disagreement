# Disputed reputable opinion

- **Domain:** Epistemic
- **When:** Aristotle
- **Who:** Aristotle
- **Remedy implied:** Dialectic, rhetoric, the method of endoxa (Nicomachean Ethics VII.1).
- **Methods presupposing it:** [Aristotelian dialectic](../approaches/aristotelian_dialectic.md), [Deliberative rhetoric](../approaches/rhetoric.md), [Jadal & munāẓara](../approaches/munazara.md), [Toulmin model](../approaches/toulmin.md)

Some questions belong to things that ‘admit of being otherwise’, where only probable premises are available. Reputable opinions conflict, and working through the puzzles (diaporēsai) on both sides is how one approaches first principles. Disagreement is the normal texture of practical and dialectical reasoning, not a sign that someone has blundered.

> “For those who wish to get clear of difficulties it is advantageous to discuss the difficulties well.” — Aristotle, Metaphysics III.1, 995a27–28
