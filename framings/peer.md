# Higher-order evidence

- **Domain:** Epistemic
- **When:** Kelly 2005; Christensen 2007; Elga 2007
- **Who:** Thomas Kelly; David Christensen; Adam Elga
- **Remedy implied:** Calibrated humility; or principled steadfastness.
- **Methods presupposing it:** [Delphi method](../approaches/delphi.md)

Suppose you learn that an epistemic peer, someone as informed and as able as you, disagrees with you. Epistemologists ask whether you should then move toward their view. Conciliationists (Christensen, Elga) say yes: ‘equal weight’. Steadfast views (Kelly) say the first-order evidence still counts. On this view, the disagreement counts as evidence, including evidence about your own reliability.
