# Divergent priors & models

- **Domain:** Epistemic
- **When:** Aumann 1976; Cowen & Hanson 2004
- **Who:** Robert Aumann; Robin Hanson; Tyler Cowen; CFAR
- **Remedy implied:** Share evidence; locate cruxes; bet; update.
- **Methods presupposing it:** [Adversarial collaboration](../approaches/adv_collab.md), [Ideological Turing test & steelmanning](../approaches/itt.md), [Double crux](../approaches/double_crux.md)

Bayesian agents with common priors whose posteriors are common knowledge cannot agree to disagree. Persistent disagreement implies different priors, private information that has not been shared, or something other than honest truth-seeking. Cowen and Hanson conclude that most disagreements are dishonest, because each party gives its own judgment more weight than it should. The practical version breaks the dispute down into world-models and finds the crux.
