# Error & ignorance

- **Domain:** Epistemic
- **When:** Socrates onward
- **Who:** Plato; Aquinas; most of the rationalist tradition
- **Remedy implied:** Inquiry, refutation, instruction.
- **Methods presupposing it:** [Socratic elenchus](../approaches/elenchus.md), [Platonic dialectic](../approaches/platonic_dialectic.md), [Stasis theory](../approaches/stasis.md), [Abhidharma controversy](../approaches/kathavatthu.md), [Vāda (good-faith debate)](../approaches/vada.md), [Tibetan monastic debate](../approaches/tibetan.md), [Mohist disputation](../approaches/bian.md), [Jadal & munāẓara](../approaches/munazara.md), [Scholastic disputation](../approaches/disputatio.md), [Experimental matter of fact](../approaches/experimental.md), [Peer review](../approaches/peer_review.md), [Street epistemology](../approaches/street_epist.md), [Toulmin model](../approaches/toulmin.md), [Formal dialectic & dialogue types](../approaches/formal_dialectic.md), [Pragma-dialectics](../approaches/pragma.md), [Adversarial collaboration](../approaches/adv_collab.md)

Two contradictory claims cannot both be true, so at least one party is wrong. On this view, disagreement is a symptom of error, and error is a lack of knowledge. Socratic intellectualism adds that no one errs willingly. Correct the belief and the dispute dissolves. Most people assume this account without stating it.

> “No one goes willingly toward the bad.” — Plato, Protagoras 358c–d
