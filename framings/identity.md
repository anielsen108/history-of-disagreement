# Identity

- **Domain:** Relational
- **When:** Tajfel & Turner 1979; Stone et al. 1999
- **Who:** Henri Tajfel; John Turner; Douglas Stone
- **Remedy implied:** Decouple claim from identity; affirm before challenging.
- **Methods presupposing it:** [Interactive problem-solving workshop](../approaches/problem_solving.md), [Difficult conversations](../approaches/difficult.md), [Reflective structured dialogue](../approaches/public_conv.md), [Deep canvassing](../approaches/deep_canvass.md)

People derive self-esteem from group membership, and intergroup comparison drives conflict even over trivial distinctions (the minimal-group paradigm). In dyads, the ‘identity conversation’ asks what being wrong would say about me. To concede the point is to lose the self.

## Sources
- Tajfel, Henri, and John C. Turner. “An Integrative Theory of Intergroup Conflict.” In *The Social Psychology of Intergroup Relations*, edited by William G. Austin and Stephen Worchel, 33–47. Monterey, CA: Brooks/Cole, 1979.
- Stone, Douglas, Bruce Patton, and Sheila Heen. *Difficult Conversations: How to Discuss What Matters Most*. New York: Viking, 1999.
