# Contest & honour

- **Domain:** Power
- **When:** archaic and classical Greece onward
- **Who:** Greek agonistic culture; the sophists; Schopenhauer
- **Remedy implied:** Rules, judges, audiences; or withdrawal from the arena.
- **Methods presupposing it:** [Antilogic](../approaches/antilogic.md), [Eristic](../approaches/eristic.md), [Deliberative rhetoric](../approaches/rhetoric.md), [Jalpa & vitaṇḍā](../approaches/jalpa.md), [Adversarial trial & cross-examination](../approaches/adversarial.md), [Competitive debate](../approaches/debate.md), [Eristic dialectic](../approaches/schopenhauer.md)

Argument is a form of combat whose stake is standing. The contest is good in itself as an arena of excellence (Hesiod’s ‘good Eris’). In its degenerate form it is vanity defended by any means. What is at issue is not the proposition but who prevails before an audience.
