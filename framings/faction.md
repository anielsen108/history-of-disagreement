# Faction & party

- **Domain:** Interest
- **When:** Hume 1741; Madison 1787
- **Who:** David Hume; James Madison
- **Remedy implied:** Institutional design that multiplies and balances factions.
- **Methods presupposing it:** [Court policy debate](../approaches/saltiron.md), [Parliamentary debate](../approaches/parliament.md)

Hume sorts real factions into those from interest, those from principle (‘abstract speculative principle’, which he finds peculiar to modern times) and those from affection. Madison: the ‘latent causes of faction are thus sown in the nature of man’, and ‘the most common and durable source’ is the unequal distribution of property. These causes cannot be removed, so their effects must be controlled by an extended republic.
