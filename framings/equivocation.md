# Equivocation & verbal dispute

- **Domain:** Linguistic
- **When:** Aristotle; Abelard; James 1907; Chalmers 2011
- **Who:** Aristotle; Peter Abelard; William James; David Chalmers
- **Remedy implied:** Distinguo; taboo the word; restate without the term.
- **Methods presupposing it:** [Aristotelian dialectic](../approaches/aristotelian_dialectic.md), [Stasis theory](../approaches/stasis.md), [Jadal & munāẓara](../approaches/munazara.md), [Scholastic disputation](../approaches/disputatio.md)

The parties use one word in two senses and so do not contradict each other. Aristotle classes homonymy first among the fallacies dependent on language. Abelard reconciles authorities by distinguishing senses. In James’s squirrel case, whether a man ‘goes round’ a squirrel depends on what ‘round’ means. Chalmers proposes the ‘method of elimination’: ban the contested word and see whether a dispute remains.
