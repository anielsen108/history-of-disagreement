Method & caveats

About this taxonomy

The two axes

Methods are classified by telos, meaning what the exchange is for. Methods with the same moves can serve different ends. Vāda and jalpa share the five-membered syllogism and differ only in whether truth or victory is sought. Prasaṅga and vitaṇḍā are formally the same act, refutation without counter-thesis, but one aims at liberation and the other at defeat. Classifying by telos rather than by technique lets those homologies show.

Views are classified by domain, meaning where the disagreement is said to live: in belief, in language, in value, in interest, in power, in the psyche, in the relationship, in one’s relation to the absolute, or nowhere problematic at all (the generative views, for which strife is the engine and not the fault).

Methods are also grouped by family, the lineage or institutional milieu, for the lineage map. The family is a historian’s grouping. The telos is a philosopher’s grouping. They cut across each other on purpose.

Conventions

Dates. Each item carries a plotting year and an honest range. Ancient Indian and Chinese dates are contested by centuries. The Nyāya-sūtra is placed at c. 150 CE and the Aṭṭhakavagga at c. 400 BCE as conventional midpoints, not claims.

Lineage. An edge means “descends from or defines itself against”. Hostile descent is still descent. The experimental matter of fact is unthinkable without the disputation it was built to escape.

Presupposition. A method “presupposes” a view if its procedure only makes sense on that diagnosis. One view per method is marked primary. These assignments are arguments, not facts, and the most contestable are flagged in the entries’ notes.

Quotations come from the cited translation. Where an attribution is weak (for instance, “the disagreement of my community is a mercy”), the entry says so.

Known gaps

Coverage after 1500 is weighted toward the Anglophone world. Practices not yet entered include Haudenosaunee condolence and council procedure, Ifá divination as a dispute-ending authority, Japanese nemawashi, Byzantine theological synods, Persian munāẓara poetry (the debate-poem genre), Hawaiian hoʻoponopono, the Truth and Reconciliation Commissions, and the Chinese examination essay. Each deserves an entry, and each would test the axes.

A caution

A taxonomy of disagreement is itself a position inside the disagreement it classifies. This one favours decomposition: find the locus, separate the strands, name the domain. That is the move shared by stasis theory, the distinguo, Chalmers’ elimination and the double crux. A Zhuangzian would decline the chart. A Mouffean would note that a chart which makes every conflict legible as a misplaced category has quietly sided with consensus. The map is offered as a tool, not a verdict.
