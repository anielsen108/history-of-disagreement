The same corpus is drawn four ways. The two-column diagram shows which diagnosis each method presupposes. The lineage map shows who descends from whom. The table spells out every link by name. The last tab shows how the mix of diagnoses shifted over time.

Click chips to hide or show a group. The address bar keeps the current filters, so you can share the exact view.

Threads are coloured by the method’s telos. Within each domain, views are ordered by the average height of the methods that use them, which cuts down crossings. Dashed threads mark primary pairings that scholars contest. Hover a group heading to light a whole telos or domain. Click a name to focus it; click again, or press “Read entry”, to open it. The slider hides methods and views dated after the chosen year, so pressing Play builds the field century by century.

One lane per family of practice. An edge means the later method descends from the earlier one or defines itself against it, as the elenchus against antilogic or the experimental matter of fact against the disputation. Hover to light a method’s full ancestry and descent. Click the legend to hide teloi. Scroll sideways on small screens.

Telos × domain

Each bar is one period; its segments give the share of that period’s methods whose diagnosis falls in each domain. Some periods hold only a few methods (one, for 1900–1950), so read their percentages as rough. Two things still show. Before 1800 no method locates disagreement in the psyche or in the relationship; epistemic, linguistic, power and contemplative diagnoses fill most of it. From 1950 to 1980 the psyche, interest and relational domains together hold seven methods in ten. After 1980 epistemic diagnoses recover with the rationalist and deliberative practices, but relational ones keep a fifth of the field. That is the “therapeutic turn” discussed on the overview, and its partial reversal.
