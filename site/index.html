A field guide, 500 BCE to the present

How people disagree, and what they think disagreement is

This site asks two linked questions. The first is how people have conducted conversations in which they disagree. The second is what they took the disagreement to be really about.

The questions are linked because every procedure assumes a diagnosis. The Socratic elenchus presupposes that the interlocutor’s beliefs are mutually inconsistent. Principled negotiation presupposes that positions hide compatible interests. Nonviolent Communication presupposes that needs never conflict and that only strategies do. The double crux presupposes that the dispute lives in a world-model with a testable node. Whoever adopts the procedure accepts its diagnosis, usually without noticing.

The diagram below makes that commitment visible. Methods sit on the left, grouped by telos, meaning what the exchange is for. Views sit on the right, grouped by domain, meaning where the trouble is said to live. Each thread joins a method to a view it presupposes; the heavier thread marks the primary one. Hover to trace a method. Click a name to focus it, so that only its threads remain; click again or press “Read entry” to open it. Dashed threads mark pairings that scholars contest. Views marked “no method”, with a hollow dot, are diagnoses that no method in this survey takes as its premise.

Three findings the data supports

1. The oldest taxonomy is still the best triage. Hermagoras’ stasis theory (2nd c. BCE) told an advocate to locate a dispute before arguing it. The advocate asked whether something happened (fact), what to call it (definition), whether it was justified (quality), and who gets to decide (jurisdiction). These four map almost exactly onto four of the nine domains used here: epistemic, linguistic, axiological and power. Much modern advice, such as Chalmers’ elimination test or the search for a crux, reinvents stasis theory.

2. There was a therapeutic turn, then a partial epistemic counter-revolution. Classify each method by its primary presupposition and count by era. Before 1500, about half of all methods locate disagreement in belief, and none locate it in the psyche or in the relationship. Between 1900 and 1975, half of all new methods locate it in the psyche or the relationship (Rogers, Burton, Watzlawick’s heirs). After 1975 the epistemic share recovers, carried by argumentation theory, deliberative polling and rationalist practices such as adversarial collaboration and the double crux. The sample is curated, so treat this as a rough orientation. It is not a measurement.

Share of methods founded in each era, by the domain of their primary presupposition. n = number of methods in the era.

3. Some diagnoses have no therapy. Thirteen of the views here are presupposed by no method in this survey. They include incommensurable paradigms, the differend, mimetic rivalry, the narcissism of small differences, sacred values, class interest and Kegan’s developmental account. Each either awaits its practice or implies that no conversational practice can reach it. Kuhn, Lyotard and Girard each say that argument stops at some limit. There Kuhn’s scientist converts, Lyotard’s wronged party bears witness, and Girard’s rivals must refuse the scapegoat. Past that limit this map has nothing to offer.
