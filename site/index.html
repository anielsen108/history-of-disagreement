A field guide, 500 BCE to the present

How people disagree, and what they think disagreement is

This site asks two linked questions. The first is how people have conducted conversations in which they disagree. The second is what they took the disagreement to be really about.

Thesis. Every method of disagreement assumes an answer to what the disagreement is about, so choosing a method is already a diagnosis. When the parties hold different diagnoses, each one’s method looks like evasion to the other. This site maps the methods, the diagnoses and the links between them, so that a reader can see which assumption a method brings.

The map in brief

The questions are linked because every method assumes a diagnosis. The Socratic elenchus presupposes that the interlocutor’s beliefs are mutually inconsistent. Principled negotiation presupposes that positions hide compatible interests. Nonviolent Communication presupposes that needs never conflict and that only strategies do. The double crux presupposes that the dispute lives in a world-model with a testable node. Whoever adopts the method accepts its diagnosis, usually without noticing.

The diagram below makes that commitment visible. One column holds the methods, grouped by aim: what the exchange is for. The traditions call this the telos. The other column holds the views, grouped by where they say the dispute lives. This site calls that the domain. A square marks a method or an aim, and a circle marks a view or a domain.

The diagram opens with every group shut. A band joins an aim to a domain when methods with that aim take a view in that domain as their primary diagnosis, and a wider band carries more methods. What the data shows

1. The oldest taxonomy is still the best triage. Hermagoras’ stasis theory (2nd c. BCE) told an advocate to locate a dispute before arguing it. The advocate asked whether something happened (fact), what to call it (definition), whether it was justified (quality), and who gets to decide (jurisdiction). These four map almost exactly onto four of the nine domains used here: epistemic, linguistic, axiological and power. Much modern advice, such as Chalmers’ elimination test or the search for a crux, reinvents stasis theory. The fit has limits. Stasis theory has no question for the other five domains, so it cannot place a quarrel driven by fear, pride, a damaged relationship, competing interests or attachment to being right.

2. There was a therapeutic turn, and it has not been reversed. Classify each method by its primary view and count by era. Before 1500, 13 of 27 methods (48%) locate disagreement in belief, and none puts the psyche or the relationship first. Between 1900 and 1975, 5 of 11 new methods do (Rogers, Rapoport, Burton, restorative justice and NVC). After 1975 the two stand level. Seven of 20 methods (35%) start from belief, carried by argumentation theory, deliberative polling and rationalist methods such as adversarial collaboration and the double crux. Seven more start from the psyche or the relationship.

The older traditions were not blind to the psyche. Five methods before 1500 name it as a secondary concern: eristic and the Aṭṭhakavagga treat vanity, and the elenchus, vāda and machloket judge the disputants’ character. What changed after 1900 is that the psyche became the primary diagnosis. The sample is curated, so the numbers give a rough orientation and do not measure the field.

3. Some diagnoses have no method. No method in this survey presupposes some of the views. They include incommensurable paradigms, mimetic rivalry, the narcissism of small differences, sacred values, class interest and Kegan’s developmental account. Each either awaits its method or implies that no conversational method can reach it. Atran and Axelrod’s symbolic concessions come close to a method for sacred values. Kuhn and Girard each say that argument stops at some limit. There Kuhn’s scientist converts, and Girard’s rivals must refuse the scapegoat. A gap can also mean that the survey is incomplete. Three closed when the Delphi method, the devil’s advocate and the truth commission were entered.
