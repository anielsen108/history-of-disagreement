# Free conversation

*l’art de conférer*

- **When:** Montaigne, *Essais* III.8, 1588
- **Who:** Michel de Montaigne
- **Telos:** Understanding
- **Family:** Early-modern civic & scientific
- **Presupposes:** [Partial perspectives](../framings/perspectival.md) (primary), [Vanity & obstinacy](../framings/vanity.md)
- **Descends from:** [Arguing both sides](utramque.md), [Pyrrhonian equipollence](pyrrhonian.md)
- **Gave rise to:** [Coffeehouse & public sphere](coffeehouse.md)

Montaigne calls conversation ‘the most fruitful and natural exercise of our mind’. He welcomes contradiction: ‘when someone opposes me, he arouses my attention, not my anger’. What he hates is obstinacy and the scholastic manner that cares more for forms than for truth. For Montaigne, disagreement is a pleasure shared by well-made minds. The salon and the ideal of the honnête homme took up this model.

## Procedure
1. Welcome correction
2. Prefer order and liveliness to form
3. Yield gracefully when beaten

## What it assumes
[Partial perspectives](../framings/perspectival.md): Each party sees a real aspect of something larger.

## In practice
- **Third party:** None.
- **Ends well when:** Both minds exercised; pleasure in the encounter.
- **How it fails:** Ease without conclusion; a gentleman’s game.

**Strongest critique** (Pascal, Entretien avec M. de Sacy (1655)): Montaigne’s easy doubt rests comfortably on a pillow of ignorance. It never faces the question that matters.
