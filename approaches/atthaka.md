# Refusing dispute

*Aṭṭhakavagga; kalaha-vivāda (quarrels and disputes)*

- **When:** among the oldest strata of the Pāli canon
- **Who:** The Buddha of the Aṭṭhakavagga (Sutta Nipāta, ch. 4)
- **Telos:** Release
- **Family:** South Asian
- **Presupposes:** [Attachment to views](../framings/attachment.md) (primary), [Vanity & obstinacy](../framings/vanity.md)

- **Gave rise to:** [Abhidharma controversy](kathavatthu.md)

The sage does not enter disputes. Those who say ‘only this is pure’ and call the other a fool are bound by their views (diṭṭhi), and their debates yield only praise or blame and the anxiety of losing. Quarrels arise from what is held dear, and what is held dear from desire. Gómez reads this as an early form of the Madhyamaka stance, which treats holding a view as the illness and offers no better view as a cure.

## Procedure
1. Decline to adopt a view to defend
2. Observe the craving behind the position
3. Let the dispute pass without taking a side

## What it assumes
[Attachment to views](../framings/attachment.md): Suffering in dispute comes from grasping at views (diṭṭhi-upādāna).

## In practice
- **Third party:** None.
- **Ends well when:** The sage does not take the dispute up.
- **How it fails:** Error goes unchallenged; quietism.

**Strongest critique** (Nyāya-sūtra 4.2.50): To protect true knowledge one must sometimes dispute, as a hedge of thorns protects a seedling.


## Whose reading?
- *consensus*: The Aṭṭhakavagga belongs to the oldest stratum of the Pāli canon. K. R. Norman, Pāli Literature (1983).
- *reading*: It preserves an anti-doctrinal strand that Madhyamaka later developed. Luis O. Gómez, ‘Proto-Mādhyamika in the Pāli Canon’, Philosophy East and West 26 (1976).
