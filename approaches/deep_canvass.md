# Deep canvassing

*non-judgmental exchange of narratives*

- **When:** Broockman & Kalla 2016; 2020
- **Who:** Leadership LAB (Los Angeles LGBT Center); David Broockman; Joshua Kalla
- **Telos:** Understanding
- **Family:** Therapeutic & dialogic
- **Presupposes:** [Threat & defensiveness](../framings/threat.md) (primary), [Identity](../framings/identity.md)
- **Descends from:** [Motivational interviewing](mi.md), [Rogerian exchange](rogerian.md)


Canvassers hold ten-minute door-step conversations. They ask for the voter’s view, listen non-judgmentally, exchange personal stories, and invite the voter to reflect on a time they were judged. Field experiments found durable attitude change. An earlier study of the method was fabricated (LaCour & Green, 2014, retracted 2015). The genuine replication that followed carries extra weight.

## Procedure
1. Elicit their view and rating
2. Exchange stories instead of arguments
3. Invite reflection; re-ask the rating

## What it assumes
[Threat & defensiveness](../framings/threat.md): A person who feels threatened cannot hear.

## In practice
- **Third party:** None.
- **Ends well when:** Measurable, durable attitude change.
- **How it fails:** Effects smaller and less general than hoped.

**Strongest critique** (Replication literature (incl. Kalla & Broockman 2020)): Effects vary by issue and context. The early retracted study inflated expectations.
