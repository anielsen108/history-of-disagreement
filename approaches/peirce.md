# Community of inquiry

*fixation of belief*

- **When:** Peirce 1877; Dewey 1938
- **Who:** C. S. Peirce; John Dewey
- **Telos:** Truth
- **Family:** Rationalist & experimental
- **Presupposes:** [Unfinished inquiry](../framings/inquiry.md) (primary)
- **Descends from:** [Experimental matter of fact](experimental.md)
- **Gave rise to:** [Discourse ethics](discourse_ethics.md)

Doubt is an irritation that inquiry relieves. Peirce ranks four ways of fixing belief: tenacity, authority, the a priori method, and science. Only science lets ‘some external permanency’ settle opinion, so that all who investigate will in the long run converge. For Peirce, disagreement marks an inquiry that is not yet finished. The remedy is more inquiry, better designed.

## Procedure
1. Convert dispute into a question with an investigable answer
2. Design the test
3. Defer final verdict to the community in the long run

## What it assumes
[Unfinished inquiry](../framings/inquiry.md): On this view, a community disagrees because its inquiry has not yet converged.

## In practice
- **Third party:** Reality, through the community of investigators.
- **Ends well when:** Convergence in the long run.
- **How it fails:** ‘The long run’ may never come for this dispute.

**Strongest critique** (Richard Rorty, Consequences of Pragmatism (1982)): Nothing guarantees convergence. ‘What inquiry would end in’ adds nothing to ‘what we now justify’.
