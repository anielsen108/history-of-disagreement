# Platonic dialectic

*dialektikē; synagōgē & diairesis*

- **When:** c. 380–350 BCE
- **Who:** Plato (Republic, Phaedrus, Sophist, Statesman)
- **Telos:** Truth
- **Family:** Greek & Roman
- **Presupposes:** [Error & ignorance](../framings/error.md) (primary), [Inconsistency & aporia](../framings/aporia.md)
- **Descends from:** [Socratic elenchus](elenchus.md)
- **Gave rise to:** [Aristotelian dialectic](aristotelian_dialectic.md), [Speculative dialectic](hegel.md)

In the middle and late dialogues, dialectic becomes the capstone discipline. It runs by hypothesis upward to an unhypothetical first principle, and by collection and division: gather scattered instances under one form, then cut that form ‘at its natural joints’. Disagreement here is a symptom of failure to see the forms. The *Republic* bars dialectic to the young, who use argument like puppies tearing at whatever is near.

## Moves
- Collect instances under one idea
- Divide along natural joints
- Test hypotheses by their consequences
