# Ideological Turing test & steelmanning

- **When:** Caplan 2011
- **Who:** Bryan Caplan; rationalist blogosphere
- **Telos:** Understanding
- **Family:** Rationalist & experimental
- **Presupposes:** [Divergent priors & models](../framings/priors.md) (primary), [Partial perspectives](../framings/perspectival.md)
- **Descends from:** [Free and open encounter](free_discussion.md), [Rapoport’s rules & region of validity](rapoport.md)
- **Gave rise to:** [Double crux](double_crux.md)

Caplan’s test asks whether you can state your opponent’s view so well that their own side cannot tell you from a true believer. It builds on Mill’s ‘know the other side’ and measures understanding by indistinguishability. Steelmanning asks for the strongest version, which may be stronger than the one its holders actually hold. At that point charity can turn into ventriloquism.

## Procedure
1. Write the opposing position
2. Have its adherents judge whether it passes

## What it assumes
[Divergent priors & models](../framings/priors.md): Bayesian agents with common priors whose posteriors are common knowledge cannot agree to disagree.

## In practice
- **Third party:** Judges from the other side.
- **Ends well when:** Indistinguishability from a true believer.
- **How it fails:** Mimicry without understanding.

**Strongest critique** (Critics of the test): One can learn a vocabulary well enough to pass without sharing the form of life that gives it sense.
