# Vāda (good-faith debate)

*vāda; sandhāya sambhāṣā*

- **When:** Nyāya-sūtra c. 2nd c. CE; Caraka-saṃhitā
- **Who:** Akṣapāda Gautama; Vātsyāyana; the Caraka-saṃhitā
- **Telos:** Truth
- **Family:** South Asian
- **Presupposes:** [Error & ignorance](../framings/error.md) (primary), [Character & motive](../framings/character.md)

- **Gave rise to:** [Jalpa & vitaṇḍā](jalpa.md), [Tibetan monastic debate](tibetan.md)

The Nyāya-sūtra defines vāda as debate between parties who use the means of knowledge (pramāṇa) and the five-membered syllogism to establish or refute, without departing from an accepted doctrine. It is debate among teacher, pupil and peer, aimed at truth. The Caraka-saṃhitā likewise separates friendly discussion (sandhāya sambhāṣā) from hostile disputation and gives etiquette for each.

## Procedure
1. Thesis, reason, example, application, conclusion
2. Rely only on pramāṇas
3. Accept points of defeat (nigrahasthāna) honestly

## What it assumes
[Error & ignorance](../framings/error.md): Two contradictory claims cannot both be true, so at least one party is wrong.

## In practice
- **Third party:** Sometimes judges or a moderator (madhyastha) in public debate.
- **Ends well when:** The thesis is established or refuted by pramāṇa.
- **How it fails:** Agreement on the means of knowledge is assumed without argument.

**Strongest critique** (Nāgārjuna, Vigrahavyāvartanī vv. 30–51): If the means of knowledge establish objects, something must establish the means of knowledge, and the regress has no end.


## Whose reading?
- *attested*: Vāda is defined as debate that uses the means of knowledge and the five-membered syllogism. Nyāya-sūtra 1.2.1.
- *consensus*: The sūtra text took shape over several centuries, between about the 2nd c. BCE and the 2nd c. CE. Matthew Dasti and Stephen Phillips, The Nyāya-sūtra: Selections (2017), introduction.
