# Pyrrhonian equipollence

*isostheneia → epochē → ataraxia*

- **When:** Pyrrho c. 300 BCE; Aenesidemus 1st c. BCE; Sextus c. 200 CE
- **Who:** Sextus Empiricus
- **Telos:** Release
- **Family:** Greek & Roman
- **Presupposes:** [Undecidability](../framings/equipollence.md) (primary), [Attachment to views](../framings/attachment.md)
- **Descends from:** [Arguing both sides](utramque.md), [Antilogic](antilogic.md)
- **Gave rise to:** [Free conversation](conference.md)

Scepticism is ‘an ability to set out oppositions among things that appear and are thought of in any way at all’. Equal force on both sides yields suspension of judgment, and tranquillity follows ‘like a shadow’. Disagreement (diaphōnia) is the first of Agrippa’s five modes. When philosophers and ordinary people dispute with no way to decide, the sceptic suspends judgment and picks no side.

## Procedure
1. Oppose appearance to appearance, thought to thought
2. Invoke the modes (dispute, regress, relativity, hypothesis, circularity)
3. Suspend; live by appearances

## What it assumes
[Undecidability](../framings/equipollence.md): Some disputes are interminable because the arguments on each side are of equal force (isostheneia).

## In practice
- **Third party:** None.
- **Ends well when:** Suspension of judgment, and with it tranquillity.
- **How it fails:** Practical paralysis, or covert dogmatism about suspension.

**Strongest critique** (Hume, Enquiry concerning Human Understanding XII (1748)): Pyrrhonism cannot survive action. Nature is too strong for principle, and the sceptic must still eat, act and believe.


## Whose reading?
- *attested*: Scepticism is an ability to set out oppositions among appearances and thoughts. Sextus Empiricus, Outlines of Pyrrhonism 1.8.
- *reading*: Pyrrho himself held a different, metaphysical doctrine, and Sextus’ scepticism owes him little beyond the name. Richard Bett, Pyrrho, His Antecedents, and His Legacy (2000).
