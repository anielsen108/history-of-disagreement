# Coffeehouse & public sphere

*Öffentlichkeit*

- **When:** first London coffeehouse 1652; peak c. 1680–1730
- **Who:** London coffeehouses; Parisian salons; German Tischgesellschaften
- **Telos:** Understanding
- **Family:** Early-modern civic & scientific
- **Presupposes:** [Collision with error](../framings/collision.md) (primary), [Contested validity claims](../framings/validity.md)
- **Descends from:** [Free conversation](conference.md)
- **Gave rise to:** [Discourse ethics](discourse_ethics.md)

On Habermas’s account, private people came together as a public to argue about matters of common concern. Status was bracketed, at least in principle, and only the argument counted. The penny university and the periodical press formed one circuit. Critics note how much was excluded, but the ideal of rational-critical debate among equals sets the norm for later deliberative theory.

## Moves
- Bracket rank
- Argue in public about common concerns
- Circulate the argument in print
