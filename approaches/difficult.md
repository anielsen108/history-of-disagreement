# Difficult conversations

*the three conversations*

- **When:** Stone, Patton & Heen, 1999
- **Who:** Douglas Stone; Bruce Patton; Sheila Heen
- **Telos:** Understanding
- **Family:** Negotiation, mediation & peacemaking
- **Presupposes:** [Identity](../framings/identity.md) (primary), [Relationship-level message](../framings/relationship_level.md)
- **Descends from:** [Principled negotiation](principled.md)


Every hard conversation is three conversations at once. The ‘what happened’ conversation is about truth, intent and blame. The feelings conversation is about what each person feels. The identity conversation is about what the conflict says about me, for instance whether I am competent, good and lovable. Shift from certainty to curiosity, from blame to contribution, from inferred intent to reported impact.

## Procedure
1. Disentangle intent from impact
2. Map joint contribution instead of blame
3. Name the identity quake

## What it assumes
[Identity](../framings/identity.md): People derive self-esteem from group membership, and intergroup comparison drives conflict even over trivial distinctions (the minimal-group paradigm).

## In practice
- **Third party:** None.
- **Ends well when:** Each person understands the three conversations; a way forward.
- **How it fails:** Treats structural conflict as interpersonal.

**Strongest critique** (Laura Nader’s critique of ‘harmony ideology’ (1990), applied here): Reframing conflict as perception and identity can personalise what is really about position and resources.



## Sources
