# Street epistemology

- **When:** Boghossian 2013; Magnabosco et al.
- **Who:** Peter Boghossian; Anthony Magnabosco
- **Telos:** Truth
- **Family:** Therapeutic & dialogic
- **Presupposes:** [Error & ignorance](../framings/error.md) (primary), [Inconsistency & aporia](../framings/aporia.md)
- **Descends from:** [Socratic elenchus](elenchus.md), [Motivational interviewing](mi.md)


This is the elenchus crossed with motivational interviewing. The questioner examines how a belief is held more than the belief itself. They ask for a confidence level and explore what would change it. It is openly persuasive in intent, though practitioners stress rapport and the interlocutor’s autonomy.

## Procedure
1. Identify a claim and a confidence level
2. Ask how they know
3. Ask what would lower confidence

## What it assumes
[Error & ignorance](../framings/error.md): Two contradictory claims cannot both be true, so at least one party is wrong.

## In practice
- **Third party:** None.
- **Ends well when:** The interlocutor lowers their confidence or examines their method.
- **How it fails:** Persuasion disguised as inquiry.

**Strongest critique** (Critics of ‘Socratic’ persuasion): If you already know where you want the other to end up, your questions are a technique and no longer an inquiry.
