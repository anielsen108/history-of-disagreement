# Double crux

- **When:** CFAR; Sabien 2017
- **Who:** Center for Applied Rationality; Duncan Sabien
- **Telos:** Truth
- **Family:** Rationalist & experimental
- **Presupposes:** [Divergent priors & models](../framings/priors.md) (primary)
- **Descends from:** [Calculemus](calculemus.md), [Ideological Turing test & steelmanning](itt.md)


Each party finds their crux, a belief that, if it changed, would change their conclusion. Then they look for a double crux: a single empirical or conceptual question on which both conclusions depend in opposite directions. The parties move the dispute down the graph of their beliefs until they reach something testable. This assumes disagreement is a matter of models and that both parties are trying to be right. Aumann’s theorem stands in the background.

## Procedure
1. State your conclusion and confidence
2. Find your crux: ‘if B were false, would I change my mind?’
3. Search for a shared crux
4. Investigate the crux

## What it assumes
[Divergent priors & models](../framings/priors.md): Bayesian agents with common priors whose posteriors are common knowledge cannot agree to disagree.

## In practice
- **Third party:** Optional facilitator.
- **Ends well when:** A shared crux identified and investigated.
- **How it fails:** Value and identity disputes have no crux.

**Strongest critique** (From the standpoint of value pluralism (Weber, Berlin)): Not every disagreement bottoms out in a testable belief. Some end at incompatible goods.
