# Adversarial collaboration

- **When:** Mellers, Hertwig & Kahneman 2001
- **Who:** Daniel Kahneman and collaborators
- **Telos:** Truth
- **Family:** Rationalist & experimental
- **Presupposes:** [Divergent priors & models](../framings/priors.md) (primary), [Error & ignorance](../framings/error.md)
- **Descends from:** [Experimental matter of fact](experimental.md)


Scientists who disagree co-design a study whose outcome each agrees in advance would count against their view. An arbiter holds the data. Kahneman proposed it as an alternative to the ‘angry science’ of comment and reply. It works best when the dispute can be turned into a test, and it shows how often it cannot.

## Procedure
1. Agree on the crucial experiment in advance
2. Pre-state predictions
3. Joint publication with an arbiter

## What it assumes
[Divergent priors & models](../framings/priors.md): Bayesian agents with common priors whose posteriors are common knowledge cannot agree to disagree.

## In practice
- **Third party:** An arbiter who holds the data.
- **Ends well when:** A jointly agreed result.
- **How it fails:** Few participants actually change their minds.

**Strongest critique** (Kahneman’s own reflections): Even after agreed tests, collaborators rarely updated. Commitment to a view is also commitment to a self.



## Sources
- Mellers, Barbara, Ralph Hertwig, and Daniel Kahneman. “Do Frequency Representations Eliminate Conjunction Effects? An Exercise in Adversarial Collaboration.” *Psychological Science* 12, no. 4 (2001): 269–275.
