# Rogerian exchange

*restatement rule*

- **When:** Carl Rogers, 1951–52; Young, Becker & Pike 1970
- **Who:** Carl Rogers
- **Telos:** Understanding
- **Family:** Therapeutic & dialogic
- **Presupposes:** [Threat & defensiveness](../framings/threat.md) (primary)

- **Gave rise to:** [Rapoport’s rules & region of validity](rapoport.md), [Nonviolent Communication](nvc.md), [Motivational interviewing](mi.md), [Reflective structured dialogue](public_conv.md), [Deep canvassing](deep_canvass.md)

Rogers names ‘our very natural tendency to judge, to evaluate’ as the main barrier to communication. His test is that each person may speak up for himself only after he has first restated the ideas and feelings of the previous speaker accurately, and to that speaker’s satisfaction. Rhetoricians turned this into ‘Rogerian argument’: reduce threat, state the opponent’s position fairly, find common ground, then propose.

## Moves
- Restate the other’s view to their satisfaction before replying
- Reduce perceived threat
- Locate shared ground first

## Sources
