# Bridging systems

*Pol.is; Community Notes*

- **When:** vTaiwan 2015–; Birdwatch/Community Notes 2021–
- **Who:** Colin Megill et al. (Pol.is); Audrey Tang; X/Twitter
- **Telos:** Decision
- **Family:** Deliberative democracy & platforms
- **Presupposes:** [Affective polarisation](../framings/polarization.md) (primary)
- **Descends from:** [Deliberative polling](delib_polling.md)
- **Gave rise to:** [AI-mediated deliberation](ai_mediated.md)

Participants vote agree or disagree on short statements written by one another. Dimensionality reduction maps the opinion clusters, and the system surfaces statements that win support across clusters. Community Notes publishes a fact-check only when raters who usually disagree both rate it helpful. The system models disagreement as geometry and looks for the bridging statement, not the median.

## Procedure
1. Crowd-written statements
2. Agree/disagree/pass voting; no replies
3. Cluster opinion space
4. Rank by cross-cluster agreement

## What it assumes
[Affective polarisation](../framings/polarization.md): Partisans increasingly dislike and distrust each other, often more than their policy differences warrant.

## In practice
- **Third party:** The algorithm.
- **Ends well when:** Statements that win support across clusters.
- **How it fails:** Bridging rewards blandness.

**Strongest critique** (Critics of consensus ranking): Truths held by one cluster never surface. The bridging statement is often the vaguest one.
