# Mediation

*facilitative, transformative & narrative mediation*

- **When:** Pound Conference 1976; Bush & Folger 1994; Winslade & Monk 2000
- **Who:** Frank Sander; Robert Baruch Bush & Joseph Folger; John Winslade & Gerald Monk
- **Telos:** Settlement
- **Family:** Negotiation, mediation & peacemaking
- **Presupposes:** [Interests beneath positions](../framings/interests.md) (primary), [Relationship-level message](../framings/relationship_level.md), [Harm to relationship](../framings/harm.md)
- **Descends from:** [Integration](follett.md)


Sander’s ‘multi-door courthouse’ launched modern alternative dispute resolution: send each dispute to the process that fits it. Facilitative mediation seeks interest-based settlement. Transformative mediation drops settlement as the goal and aims at empowerment and recognition, so that each party regains clarity and sees the other. Narrative mediation, drawing on Foucault, helps parties deconstruct ‘conflict-saturated stories’ and co-author alternatives.

## Moves
- Neutral third party controls process, not outcome
- Separate positions from interests (facilitative)
- Follow the parties’ shifts in empowerment and recognition (transformative)
- Externalise the conflict story (narrative)
