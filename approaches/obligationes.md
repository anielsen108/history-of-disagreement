# Obligational disputation

*obligationes*

- **When:** c. 1200–1400; Walter Burley c. 1302
- **Who:** Walter Burley; Roger Swyneshed; William of Ockham
- **Telos:** Formation
- **Family:** Latin scholastic
- **Presupposes:** [Inconsistency & aporia](../framings/aporia.md) (primary)
- **Descends from:** [Scholastic disputation](disputatio.md), [Aristotelian dialectic](aristotelian_dialectic.md)
- **Gave rise to:** [Formal dialectic & dialogue types](formal_dialectic.md)

A respondent is ‘obliged’ to maintain a proposition that may be false, called the positum. An opponent then proposes further sentences, which the respondent must grant, deny or doubt so as to keep the set consistent. The respondent loses by contradiction. The game trains the management of commitments. Catarina Dutilh Novaes reads it as an ancestor of belief-revision and dialogue logics.

## Procedure
1. Positum accepted
2. Grant what follows; deny what is incompatible; answer irrelevant sentences by their truth
3. Lose on inconsistency

## What it assumes
[Inconsistency & aporia](../framings/aporia.md): The disagreement that matters is within a person, among beliefs they already hold.

## In practice
- **Third party:** A master who sets the positum.
- **Ends well when:** The respondent survives the time limit without contradiction.
- **How it fails:** A game cut off from any real question.

**Strongest critique** (Juan Luis Vives, In pseudodialecticos (1520)): These sophistical exercises teach young men to talk nonsense with technical precision.
