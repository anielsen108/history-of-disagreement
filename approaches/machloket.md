# Dispute for the sake of Heaven

*מחלוקת לשם שמים machloket l’shem shamayim*

- **When:** Hillel & Shammai 1st c. BCE; Mishnah c. 200 CE; Bavli c. 500–600
- **Who:** The Tannaim and Amoraim
- **Telos:** Plurality
- **Family:** Rabbinic
- **Presupposes:** [Plurality willed from above](../framings/divine_plurality.md) (primary), [Character & motive](../framings/character.md)

- **Gave rise to:** [Havruta](havruta.md)

Avot 5:17: a dispute for the sake of Heaven will endure, and the model is Hillel and Shammai. A dispute not for Heaven’s sake will not endure, and the model is Korah. The Mishnah records the minority view (Eduyot 1:5) so that a later court may rely on it. In Eruvin 13b a heavenly voice declares ‘these and these are the words of the living God’, yet the law follows Hillel, because his school was gentle and taught Shammai’s view first. In the oven of Akhnai (Bava Metzia 59b) the majority overrules a heavenly voice: ‘it is not in heaven’.

## Procedure
1. Record minority opinions with attribution
2. Decide by majority without declaring the minority false
3. Judge the dispute by its motive, not its outcome
4. State the opponent’s view before your own

## What it assumes
[Plurality willed from above](../framings/divine_plurality.md): ‘These and these are the words of the living God.’

## In practice
- **Third party:** The court and the majority, which decide practice without erasing the minority.
- **Ends well when:** A ruling for practice; all views preserved for study.
- **How it fails:** Dispute for victory masquerading as dispute for Heaven (Korah).

**Strongest critique** (Sanhedrin 88b; Maimonides, Introduction to the Mishnah Commentary): Disputes multiplied when students did not serve their teachers enough. Disagreement records a loss of transmitted knowledge.


## Whose reading?
- *attested*: A heavenly voice declares ‘these and these are the words of the living God’, and the law follows Hillel. Babylonian Talmud, Eruvin 13b.
