# Jadal & munāẓara

*jadal; munāẓara; ādāb al-baḥth wa-l-munāẓara*

- **When:** 9th–14th c.; Sīrāfī–Mattā debate 932; Samarqandī d. 1303
- **Who:** Jurists and theologians; Ibn ʿAqīl; Shams al-Dīn al-Samarqandī
- **Telos:** Truth
- **Family:** Islamic
- **Presupposes:** [Error & ignorance](../framings/error.md) (primary), [Disputed reputable opinion](../framings/endoxa.md), [Equivocation & verbal dispute](../framings/equivocation.md)
- **Descends from:** [Aristotelian dialectic](aristotelian_dialectic.md)
- **Gave rise to:** [Science of juristic disagreement](khilaf.md), [Scholastic disputation](disputatio.md)

Juridical dialectic (jadal) structured legal reasoning in the madrasa through question and objection. Samarqandī’s ‘etiquette of inquiry and disputation’ later recast it as a general theory with fixed roles: claimant, questioner, and the permitted objections (manʿ, naqḍ, muʿāraḍa). The famous majlis of 932 between al-Sīrāfī and Abū Bishr Mattā was itself a dispute about what disputes are about: universal logic, or the grammar of a particular language.

## Moves
- Claimant asserts; questioner may demand proof (manʿ)
- Counter-instance (naqḍ)
- Counter-argument (muʿāraḍa)
