# Abhidharma controversy

*Kathāvatthu*

- **When:** trad. Third Council, c. 250 BCE; compiled over centuries
- **Who:** Attributed to Moggaliputta Tissa
- **Telos:** Truth
- **Family:** South Asian
- **Presupposes:** [Error & ignorance](../framings/error.md) (primary)
- **Descends from:** [Refusing dispute](atthaka.md)
- **Gave rise to:** [Prasaṅga (reductio without thesis)](prasanga.md)

This canonical text sets out over two hundred points of controversy between the Theravāda and rival schools as formalised exchanges. The orthodox questioner extracts an admission, then shows that it entails a contradiction with another admission. It is an early Indian dialogue-logic. Its structure is close to the elenchus, but the school used it to police the boundaries of doctrine.

## Procedure
1. Establish the opponent’s thesis
2. Derive the ‘refutation by implication’ (anuloma/paṭikamma)
3. Close the point with the opponent’s self-contradiction

## What it assumes
[Error & ignorance](../framings/error.md): Two contradictory claims cannot both be true, so at least one party is wrong.

## In practice
- **Third party:** The orthodox community that preserves the record.
- **Ends well when:** The dissenting thesis is shown self-contradictory.
- **How it fails:** The opponent never speaks in their own voice.

**Strongest critique** (This site’s summary of the usual caution about polemical sources): The winning side reconstructs the rival schools’ positions, so it stages the controversy.


## Whose reading?
- *consensus*: Tradition assigns the text to Moggaliputta Tissa’s council under Aśoka; specialists treat much of it as later accretion. K. R. Norman, Pāli Literature (1983).
