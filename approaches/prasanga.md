# Prasaṅga (reductio without thesis)

*prasaṅga; Vigrahavyāvartanī*

- **When:** Nāgārjuna, c. 150–250 CE
- **Who:** Nāgārjuna; later Candrakīrti
- **Telos:** Release
- **Family:** South Asian
- **Presupposes:** [Attachment to views](../framings/attachment.md) (primary), [Undecidability](../framings/equipollence.md)
- **Descends from:** [Abhidharma controversy](kathavatthu.md), [Jalpa & vitaṇḍā](jalpa.md)
- **Gave rise to:** [Tibetan monastic debate](tibetan.md)

The Mādhyamika shows that each of the opponent’s positions entails absurdity, without advancing a position of its own. Pressed by Nyāya critics, Nāgārjuna answers: ‘If I had any thesis, that fault would apply to me. But I do not have any thesis.’ In form this resembles vitaṇḍā. Its purpose is liberation, the ‘pacification of conceptual proliferation’ (prapañcopaśama).

## Procedure
1. Take the opponent’s view on its own terms
2. Draw out its unwanted consequence
3. Decline to substitute a counter-view

## What it assumes
[Attachment to views](../framings/attachment.md): Suffering in dispute comes from grasping at views (diṭṭhi-upādāna).

## In practice
- **Third party:** None.
- **Ends well when:** Every view is shown untenable; grasping ceases.
- **How it fails:** It can look like nihilism or mere destructive cavil.

**Strongest critique** (Naiyāyika objectors (Vigrahavyāvartanī vv. 1–20)): A refutation that asserts nothing refutes nothing. Your denial is also a thesis.


## Whose reading?
- *attested*: ‘If I had any thesis, that fault would apply to me.’ Nāgārjuna, Vigrahavyāvartanī 29.
- *reading*: Nāgārjuna rejects only theses that ascribe intrinsic nature (svabhāva), and not every assertion. Jan Westerhoff, Nāgārjuna’s Madhyamaka (2009), ch. 9.
