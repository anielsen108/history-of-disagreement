# Arguing both sides

*in utramque partem*

- **When:** Arcesilaus c. 265 BCE; Carneades in Rome, 155 BCE
- **Who:** Arcesilaus; Carneades; Cicero
- **Telos:** Truth
- **Family:** Greek & Roman
- **Presupposes:** [Undecidability](../framings/equipollence.md) (primary), [Partial perspectives](../framings/perspectival.md)
- **Descends from:** [Socratic elenchus](elenchus.md), [Aristotelian dialectic](aristotelian_dialectic.md)
- **Gave rise to:** [Pyrrhonian equipollence](pyrrhonian.md), [Free conversation](conference.md), [Free and open encounter](free_discussion.md)

The sceptical Academy turned Socratic questioning into a standing practice of arguing for and against any thesis. On the Athenian embassy of 155 BCE Carneades argued for justice one day and against it the next, scandalising Cato. Cicero, who admired the method, said it suits the search for what is most probable and trains the orator in copiousness.

## Moves
- Argue the thesis; argue its contrary
- Withhold assent, or assent only to the persuasive (pithanon)
