# Agonistic pluralism

*adversary, not enemy*

- **When:** Mouffe 2000, 2005
- **Who:** Chantal Mouffe
- **Telos:** Plurality
- **Family:** Deliberative democracy & platforms
- **Presupposes:** [Hegemony](../framings/hegemony.md) (primary), [Friend & enemy](../framings/friend_enemy.md)
- **Descends from:** [Parliamentary debate](parliament.md)


Against Habermasian consensus, Mouffe argues that antagonism is ineradicable and every consensus is a hegemonic settlement that excludes. The democratic task is to turn antagonism (enemy against enemy) into agonism (adversary against adversary): opponents who share allegiance to liberty and equality and fight over their interpretation. Democracies should give disagreement institutional channels and stop trying to dissolve it.

## Procedure
1. Name the conflict as political, not moral
2. Provide channels for passionate partisanship
3. Treat consensus claims with suspicion

## What it assumes
[Hegemony](../framings/hegemony.md): Every social order is a contingent hegemonic articulation, and every consensus excludes.

## In practice
- **Third party:** Democratic institutions as channels.
- **Ends well when:** None final; conflict channelled.
- **How it fails:** No criterion separates adversary from enemy.

**Strongest critique** (Deliberative democrats): Without shared standards of justification, agonism cannot say why anyone should accept defeat.


## Sources
- Mouffe, Chantal. *The Democratic Paradox*. London: Verso, 2000.
- Mouffe, Chantal. *On the Political*. London: Routledge, 2005.
