# Reflective structured dialogue

*Public Conversations Project / Essential Partners*

- **When:** Boston, 1989–; abortion dialogues 1990s
- **Who:** Laura Chasin; Richard Chasin; Margaret Herzig
- **Telos:** Understanding
- **Family:** Therapeutic & dialogic
- **Presupposes:** [Identity](../framings/identity.md) (primary), [Affective polarisation](../framings/polarization.md)
- **Descends from:** [Rogerian exchange](rogerian.md)


Family therapists applied systemic techniques to polarised public issues, starting with abortion. Participants agree to communication rules. They answer prepared questions in timed rounds, beginning with personal experience. They are asked to name uncertainties and ‘grey areas’ within their own view. Facilitators aim to break the ‘old conversation’ of stereotyped positions and to show the complexity on each side.

## Procedure
1. Agreements in advance
2. Go-rounds with timed, uninterrupted speech
3. Questions of genuine curiosity
4. Name your own grey areas

## What it assumes
[Identity](../framings/identity.md): People derive self-esteem from group membership, and intergroup comparison drives conflict even over trivial distinctions (the minimal-group paradigm).

## In practice
- **Third party:** Trained facilitators.
- **Ends well when:** Stereotypes broken; complexity visible.
- **How it fails:** Civility without change.

**Strongest critique** (Chantal Mouffe, On the Political (2005), applied here): Dialogue that lowers the temperature can depoliticise conflicts that need to be fought out.
