# Activist consensus process

*formal consensus*

- **When:** Movement for a New Society 1971; anti-nuclear and Occupy movements
- **Who:** Movement for a New Society; Clamshell Alliance; Occupy
- **Telos:** Decision
- **Family:** Communal discernment
- **Presupposes:** [Incomplete discernment](../framings/discernment.md) (primary), [Constructive difference](../framings/constructive.md)
- **Descends from:** [Sense of the meeting](quaker.md)


Activist movements secularised Quaker practice. Their consensus process uses facilitator roles, temperature checks, blocks and stand-asides. The group treats each objection as information about a weakness in the proposal. Its known pathologies are tyranny of the patient, exhaustion and the hidden veto. These inspired later hybrids (sociocracy’s ‘consent’, ‘no paramount objection’).

## Procedure
1. Proposal → clarifying questions → concerns → amendments
2. Test for consensus; record stand-asides; honour blocks

## What it assumes
[Incomplete discernment](../framings/discernment.md): Truth is one and is discerned together.

## In practice
- **Third party:** A facilitator.
- **Ends well when:** No remaining block.
- **How it fails:** Hidden elites and exhaustion; the patient minority rules.

**Strongest critique** (Jo Freeman, ‘The Tyranny of Structurelessness’ (1972)): Groups without formal structure develop informal elites who are unaccountable because invisible.
