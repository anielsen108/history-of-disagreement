# Bohmian dialogue

*suspension*

- **When:** mid-1980s; *On Dialogue* 1996
- **Who:** David Bohm
- **Telos:** Understanding
- **Family:** Therapeutic & dialogic
- **Presupposes:** [Hinges & language-games](../framings/hinges.md) (primary), [Attachment to views](../framings/attachment.md)



A group of about twenty to forty people meets without agenda or decision. Participants practise suspending their assumptions, neither acting on them nor suppressing them, so that the group can observe thought as a collective, self-sustaining process. In a disagreement, participants can watch unexamined assumptions defend themselves as if they were the self.

## Procedure
1. No agenda, no leader, no decision
2. Suspend reactions; observe them
3. Attend to the flow of meaning between participants

## What it assumes
[Hinges & language-games](../framings/hinges.md): Some propositions are hinges on which doubting turns.

## In practice
- **Third party:** Facilitator only at first; then none.
- **Ends well when:** Shared meaning; thought seen as a collective process.
- **How it fails:** Endless talk without decision.

**Strongest critique** (Practitioners of decision-oriented dialogue): Groups with real stakes cannot suspend forever. Some decisions must be made by people with conflicting interests.



## Sources
- Bohm, David. *On Dialogue*. Edited by Lee Nichol. London: Routledge, 1996.
