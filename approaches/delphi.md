# Delphi method

- **When:** RAND Corporation, 1950s; Dalkey & Helmer 1963
- **Who:** Norman Dalkey; Olaf Helmer
- **Telos:** Truth
- **Family:** Rationalist & experimental
- **Presupposes:** [Higher-order evidence](../framings/peer.md) (primary), [Vanity & obstinacy](../framings/vanity.md)



A panel of experts answers a questionnaire, each alone and without names attached. A coordinator sends back the spread of the answers, with the reasons given for the outlying ones, and the experts revise. After several rounds the coordinator reports the median and the spread that remains. Dalkey and Helmer designed the anonymity to keep rank and forceful personalities from moving the estimate. The method treats the other experts’ answers as evidence each expert should weigh.

## Procedure
1. Experts answer separately and anonymously
2. Coordinator returns the spread of answers and the reasons for outliers
3. Experts revise; repeat for several rounds
4. Report the median and the remaining spread

## What it assumes
[Higher-order evidence](../framings/peer.md): Suppose you learn that an epistemic peer, someone as informed and as able as you, disagrees with you.

## In practice
- **Third party:** A coordinator, who collects and feeds back the answers.
- **Ends well when:** The estimates converge, or the remaining spread is reported.
- **How it fails:** Panellists drift to the median they are shown.

**Strongest critique** (Harold Sackman, Delphi Critique (1975)): Delphi gathers opinions and reports their convergence as a finding. The convergence can show only that panellists moved toward the group figure.


## Sources
- Dalkey, Norman, and Olaf Helmer. “An Experimental Application of the Delphi Method to the Use of Experts.” *Management Science* 9 (1963): 458–467.
