# Principled negotiation

*Harvard Negotiation Project*

- **When:** Fisher & Ury, 1981
- **Who:** Roger Fisher; William Ury
- **Telos:** Settlement
- **Family:** Negotiation, mediation & peacemaking
- **Presupposes:** [Interests beneath positions](../framings/interests.md) (primary)
- **Descends from:** [Integration](follett.md), [Strategic bargaining](game_bargaining.md)
- **Gave rise to:** [Difficult conversations](difficult.md)

The method has four rules. Separate the people from the problem. Focus on interests, not positions. Invent options for mutual gain. Insist on objective criteria. A fifth instruction follows: know your BATNA. The central claim is that a position encodes interests with loss, and that interests are compatible more often than positions are.

## Procedure
1. Ask ‘why?’ behind each position
2. Brainstorm before evaluating
3. Agree on standards before applying them

## What it assumes
[Interests beneath positions](../framings/interests.md): What people say they want, their positions, is a strategy for what they need, their interests.

## In practice
- **Third party:** Optional.
- **Ends well when:** A wise agreement measured by objective criteria.
- **How it fails:** Ignores the hard distributive core.

**Strongest critique** (James J. White, review of Getting to Yes (1984)): Most negotiation is distributive. Fisher and Ury wish away the part where one side’s gain is the other’s loss.



## Sources
- Fisher, Roger, and William Ury. *Getting to Yes: Negotiating Agreement Without Giving In*. Boston: Houghton Mifflin, 1981.
