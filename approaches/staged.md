# Staged religious disputation

*disputatio coram rege*

- **When:** Paris 1240; Barcelona 1263; Tortosa 1413–14
- **Who:** Nahmanides vs. Pablo Christiani (Barcelona)
- **Telos:** Victory
- **Family:** Latin scholastic
- **Presupposes:** [War & sovereignty](../framings/sovereign.md) (primary), [Power / knowledge](../framings/powerknow.md)
- **Descends from:** [Scholastic disputation](disputatio.md)
- **Gave rise to:** [Sovereign arbitration](arbitration.md)

Christian authorities compelled Jewish scholars to debate Christian disputants, often converts, before a king or pope. At Paris in 1240 the Talmud itself stood accused, and copies were burned afterwards. At Barcelona in 1263 the question was whether the Talmud attests that the Messiah has come. The king granted Nahmanides freedom of speech, and the Dominicans later prosecuted him for publishing his account. The Christian side set the question and the limits of reply, so the Jewish disputant was allowed to defend and forbidden to attack.

## Procedure
1. Sovereign sets the question and the limits of speech
2. Victory declared by the stronger party

## What it assumes
[War & sovereignty](../framings/sovereign.md): Private judgment about right and wrong is the seed of civil war.

## In practice
- **Third party:** The sovereign, who sets terms and declares the winner.
- **Ends well when:** Victory declared by power.
- **How it fails:** It is not a disputation at all.

**Strongest critique** (Nahmanides, Vikkuaḥ (1263)): Speech under a king’s licence, before a hostile court, decides nothing; the speaker pays for winning.
