# Toulmin model

*claim–data–warrant*

- **When:** Toulmin 1958
- **Who:** Stephen Toulmin
- **Telos:** Truth
- **Family:** Argumentation theory
- **Presupposes:** [Error & ignorance](../framings/error.md) (primary), [Disputed reputable opinion](../framings/endoxa.md)
- **Descends from:** [Aristotelian dialectic](aristotelian_dialectic.md)
- **Gave rise to:** [Pragma-dialectics](pragma.md)

Toulmin models practical argument on the law court instead of on geometry. A claim rests on data through a warrant, which has backing and is limited by a qualifier and rebuttals. Many disagreements turn out to sit at the warrant, the inference licence, and not at the data or the claim. Warrants are ‘field-dependent’, so disputes across fields need a meta-argument about which warrants govern.

## Procedure
1. Separate claim from data
2. Surface the warrant
3. Locate the disagreement: data, warrant, backing or qualifier

## What it assumes
[Error & ignorance](../framings/error.md): Two contradictory claims cannot both be true, so at least one party is wrong.

## In practice
- **Third party:** None.
- **Ends well when:** The disagreement located at data, warrant or backing.
- **How it fails:** Field-dependence leaves cross-field disputes open.

**Strongest critique** (Formal logicians): A warrant is just a suppressed premise. Toulmin’s layout adds nothing a valid argument form lacks.



## Sources
- Toulmin, Stephen. *The Uses of Argument*. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1958.
