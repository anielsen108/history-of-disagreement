# Restorative justice & circles

*victim–offender mediation; peacemaking circle*

- **When:** Kitchener, Ontario 1974; Zehr 1990; Braithwaite 1989
- **Who:** Howard Zehr; John Braithwaite; Kay Pranis
- **Telos:** Understanding
- **Family:** Negotiation, mediation & peacemaking
- **Presupposes:** [Harm to relationship](../framings/harm.md) (primary), [Relationship-level message](../framings/relationship_level.md)

- **Gave rise to:** [Truth commission](truth_commission.md)

Restorative justice treats crime and conflict as harm to people and relationships. The legal model sees a broken rule; this model sees people who were hurt. Victim, offender and community meet in a facilitated encounter or a talking-piece circle to name the harm, take responsibility and repair. Braithwaite adds ‘reintegrative shaming’: disapproval of the act within continued respect for the person.

## Procedure
1. Talking piece; speak from experience
2. Who was harmed? What do they need? Whose obligation?
3. Agreement on repair

## What it assumes
[Harm to relationship](../framings/harm.md): Restorative justice sees conflict and crime as wounds in a web of relationships.

## In practice
- **Third party:** A facilitator or circle keeper.
- **Ends well when:** Agreement on repair; relationship restored.
- **How it fails:** Pressure on victims to forgive.

**Strongest critique** (Annalise Acorn, Compulsory Compassion (2004)): The promise of healing and reconciliation can coerce victims and sentimentalise justice.
