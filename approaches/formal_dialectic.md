# Formal dialectic & dialogue types

*commitment stores*

- **When:** Hamblin 1970; Walton & Krabbe 1995
- **Who:** C. L. Hamblin; Douglas Walton; Erik Krabbe
- **Telos:** Truth
- **Family:** Argumentation theory
- **Presupposes:** [Inconsistency & aporia](../framings/aporia.md) (primary), [Error & ignorance](../framings/error.md)
- **Descends from:** [Obligational disputation](obligationes.md), [Aristotelian dialectic](aristotelian_dialectic.md)
- **Gave rise to:** [Pragma-dialectics](pragma.md)

Hamblin modelled argument as a rule-governed game in which each move updates a participant’s commitment store, and he defined fallacies as illegal moves. Walton and Krabbe classified dialogue by initial situation and goal: persuasion, inquiry, negotiation, deliberation, information-seeking and eristic. A fallacy then often turns out to be a covert shift from one dialogue type to another.

## Moves
- Track commitments explicitly
- Name the dialogue type in play
- Flag illicit type-shifts
