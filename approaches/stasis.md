# Stasis theory

*stasis / status / constitutio*

- **When:** Hermagoras of Temnos, mid-2nd c. BCE
- **Who:** Hermagoras; Cicero; Quintilian; Hermogenes
- **Telos:** Decision
- **Family:** Greek & Roman
- **Presupposes:** [Equivocation & verbal dispute](../framings/equivocation.md) (primary), [Error & ignorance](../framings/error.md), [What cannot be measured](../framings/unmeasurable.md), [War & sovereignty](../framings/sovereign.md)
- **Descends from:** [Deliberative rhetoric](rhetoric.md)
- **Gave rise to:** [Adversarial trial & cross-examination](adversarial.md)

Before arguing a case, the advocate finds its stasis, the point at which the parties come to rest against each other. The theory sets four questions in order. Conjecture asks whether a thing was done. Definition asks what it should be called. Quality asks whether it was justified. Translation asks whether this forum has jurisdiction. Stasis theory is the oldest explicit taxonomy of what a disagreement is ‘really about’. It already separates questions of fact, of words, of value and of jurisdiction.

## Procedure
1. Identify the stasis before arguing
2. Concede earlier stases to contest later ones
3. Shift the issue (translatio) when the forum is wrong

## What it assumes
[Equivocation & verbal dispute](../framings/equivocation.md): The parties use one word in two senses and so do not contradict each other.

## In practice
- **Third party:** Judge or court, whose jurisdiction is itself one of the stases.
- **Ends well when:** The issue is located; the case is argued at the right stasis.
- **How it fails:** Assumes a shared forum and law within which the issue can be placed.

**Strongest critique** (From Lyotard’s standpoint (The Differend, 1983)): Some wrongs cannot be stated in the forum’s idiom. Stasis theory presupposes the common genre a differend lacks.



> Most quarrels are carried on at two different stases at once. One party argues fact while the other argues definition.
