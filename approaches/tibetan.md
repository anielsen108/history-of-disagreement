# Tibetan monastic debate

*rtsod pa; bsdus grwa*

- **When:** Sangphu, 12th c.; Geluk curriculum to the present
- **Who:** Chapa Chökyi Senge; Geluk monasteries (Sera, Drepung, Ganden)
- **Telos:** Formation
- **Family:** South Asian
- **Presupposes:** [Error & ignorance](../framings/error.md) (primary), [Inconsistency & aporia](../framings/aporia.md)
- **Descends from:** [Prasaṅga (reductio without thesis)](prasanga.md), [Vāda (good-faith debate)](vada.md)


A standing defender sits, and a challenger stands, claps and stamps on each point. The challenger presses consequences (thal ’gyur) built from the defender’s own answers. The defender may reply only ‘I accept’, ‘the reason is not established’ or ‘there is no pervasion’. Dreyfus calls it hermeneutics through combat. Monks learn the canon by being forced to defend it in public.

## Procedure
1. State a consequence
2. Defender answers with one of three replies
3. Clap on each point; lineage-specific gestures mark defeat

## What it assumes
[Error & ignorance](../framings/error.md): Two contradictory claims cannot both be true, so at least one party is wrong.

## In practice
- **Third party:** Assembled monks and the abbot, who watch and judge.
- **Ends well when:** The defender holds the line or is forced to accept an absurd consequence.
- **How it fails:** Virtuosity in defending a fixed curriculum takes the place of inquiry.

**Strongest critique** (Critics of scholastic monasticism (cf. Dreyfus 2003)): The courtyard trains the defence of orthodoxy, and nobody questions the canon.
