# Mohist disputation

*辯 biàn*

- **When:** Later Mohist Canons, c. 300 BCE
- **Who:** The Later Mohists
- **Telos:** Truth
- **Family:** Chinese
- **Presupposes:** [Disorder of names](../framings/names.md) (primary), [Error & ignorance](../framings/error.md)

- **Gave rise to:** [Walking two roads](liangxing.md)

Canon A74: ‘disputation is contending over converse claims’. One party says ‘it is an ox’ and the other ‘it is not’, and only one can fit (dang). Bian is a practice of distinguishing (bian 辨). It asks whether a name fits a thing, and it uses analogical procedures such as ‘parallelising’ (mou) and ‘inferring’ (tui). The Mohists rejected Zhuangzi-style relativism: someone wins every genuine dispute.

## Procedure
1. Fix the predicate under dispute
2. Draw parallels between cases (mou, tui)
3. Judge which side fits (dang)

## What it assumes
[Disorder of names](../framings/names.md): Disputes arise when names no longer track realities and roles.

## In practice
- **Third party:** None required: the fit of name to thing decides.
- **Ends well when:** One side is shown to fit (dang).
- **How it fails:** Disputes over indexical ‘this’ and ‘that’ do not resolve by fit.

**Strongest critique** (Zhuangzi, ‘Qiwulun’): Your ‘this’ is my ‘that’. No judge can stand outside both of us to say who fits.
