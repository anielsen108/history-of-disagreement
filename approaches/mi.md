# Motivational interviewing

*evoking change talk*

- **When:** Miller 1983; Miller & Rollnick 1991
- **Who:** William R. Miller; Stephen Rollnick
- **Telos:** Understanding
- **Family:** Therapeutic & dialogic
- **Presupposes:** [Threat & defensiveness](../framings/threat.md) (primary)
- **Descends from:** [Rogerian exchange](rogerian.md)
- **Gave rise to:** [Street epistemology](street_epist.md), [Deep canvassing](deep_canvass.md)

Arguing for change produces resistance, because the listener voices the other side of their own ambivalence. MI instead uses reflective listening to evoke the person’s own reasons for change, and it ‘rolls with resistance’. It is a clinical method that has become a general template for persuasion without confrontation, and it is a direct ancestor of street epistemology and deep canvassing.

## Moves
- Open questions; affirmations; reflections; summaries (OARS)
- Elicit their reasons, not yours
- Avoid the righting reflex

## Sources
- Miller, William R., and Stephen Rollnick. *Motivational Interviewing: Preparing People to Change Addictive Behavior*. New York: Guilford, 1991.
