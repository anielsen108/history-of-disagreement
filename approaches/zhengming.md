# Rectification of names

*正名 zhèngmíng*

- **When:** Analects (5th c. BCE); Xunzi ch. 22 (3rd c. BCE)
- **Who:** Confucius; Xunzi
- **Telos:** Decision
- **Family:** Chinese
- **Presupposes:** [Disorder of names](../framings/names.md) (primary), [War & sovereignty](../framings/sovereign.md)

- **Gave rise to:** [Court policy debate](saltiron.md)

‘If names are not correct, speech will not accord with things’ (Analects 13.3). For Xunzi, disordered names breed disordered disputes. The cure is a sage-king who fixes names by convention, after which the people need not dispute. Xunzi treats disagreement as a disorder of language and prescribes a political cure.

## Procedure
1. Settle terminology by authority and convention
2. Treat clever disputation that confuses names as subversion

## What it assumes
[Disorder of names](../framings/names.md): Disputes arise when names no longer track realities and roles.

## In practice
- **Third party:** The sage-king, who fixes names.
- **Ends well when:** Names are settled; disputes cease.
- **How it fails:** Semantics by decree.

**Strongest critique** (Daodejing 1): The name that can be named is not the constant name. Fixing names fixes nothing that matters.
