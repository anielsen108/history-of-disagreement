# Citizens’ assemblies

*sortition-based deliberation*

- **When:** British Columbia 2004; Ireland 2016–18
- **Who:** BC Citizens’ Assembly on Electoral Reform; Irish Citizens’ Assembly
- **Telos:** Decision
- **Family:** Deliberative democracy & platforms
- **Presupposes:** [Burdens of judgment](../framings/burdens.md) (primary), [Value pluralism](../framings/warring_gods.md)
- **Descends from:** [Deliberative polling](delib_polling.md)


A body selected by lot hears evidence, deliberates over weeks or months, and recommends. The Irish Assembly’s recommendation on the Eighth Amendment preceded the 2018 referendum. Gutmann and Thompson supply the normative frame. Moral disagreement is permanent and reasonable, so deliberation should follow rules of reciprocity, publicity and accountability and should not aim at agreement.

## Procedure
1. Sortition
2. Learning → consultation → deliberation → recommendation
3. Majority vote after deliberation

## What it assumes
[Burdens of judgment](../framings/burdens.md): Reasonable people disagree without any of them being irrational or ill-willed.

## In practice
- **Third party:** Facilitators and expert witnesses.
- **Ends well when:** A recommendation, usually to parliament or referendum.
- **How it fails:** A small group decides for millions.

**Strongest critique** (Cristina Lafont, Democracy without Shortcuts (2019)): Assemblies are a shortcut: citizens are asked to defer to a body they did not authorise.


## Sources

- Fishkin, James S. *When the People Speak: Deliberative Democracy and Public Consultation*. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2009.
