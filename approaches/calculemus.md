# Calculemus

*characteristica universalis*

- **When:** Leibniz, 1685 (after Llull’s *Ars*, c. 1305)
- **Who:** G. W. Leibniz
- **Telos:** Truth
- **Family:** Rationalist & experimental
- **Presupposes:** [Calculation error](../framings/calculation.md) (primary)
- **Descends from:** [Scholastic disputation](disputatio.md)
- **Gave rise to:** [Double crux](double_crux.md)

Leibniz imagined a universal characteristic in which reasoning would be calculation. ‘When there are disputes among persons, we can simply say: let us calculate, without further ado, to see who is right.’ A disagreement would become an error in arithmetic that anyone could find on paper. No one has built the calculus. The hope survives in decision theory, Bayesian epistemology and the double crux.

## Procedure
1. Formalise terms
2. Compute
3. Locate the error

## What it assumes
[Calculation error](../framings/calculation.md): With an adequate notation, every dispute would be a mistake in calculation, checkable like a sum.

## In practice
- **Third party:** The calculus.
- **Ends well when:** A computation shows who is right.
- **How it fails:** Most disputes do not formalise.

**Strongest critique** (Thomas Kuhn, Structure of Scientific Revolutions (1962)): There is no neutral algorithm for theory choice. Even scientists cannot calculate their way across a paradigm.
